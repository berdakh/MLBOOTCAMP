# Appendices

The appendices support the fifty-eight chapters and five capstone projects of
the book. They fall into four groups: reference material that collects what
the chapters define (glossary, equations, figures, cheat sheet); practical
instructions for running the labs (before you start, setup, Colab,
troubleshooting); material for planning and assessing study (study plan,
diagnostic guide, readiness rubric, interview questions); and a record of how
the book itself was checked (verification, solutions). Each appendix is
described in one paragraph below.

## Reference

**[Glossary](glossary.md).** Every term from the "Key terms" tables of
Parts I–III, merged into one alphabetical list. Each entry gives a short
definition, usually an example from the book's data, and a link to the
chapter where the term is taught. A preliminary section gives informal
analogies for the central ideas of each topic, together with the point at
which each analogy stops being accurate.

**[Equations](equations.md).** The key equations of all chapters, grouped by
topic (evaluation, statistics and experimentation, features, optimisation,
ranking, deep learning, scaling, serving, monitoring, advertising, privacy and
fairness, and algorithmic complexity). Each equation is stated in symbols and
in one sentence of words, with a link to the chapter that derives it.

**[List of figures](figures.md).** Every figure in the book with its caption.
The list is generated by `tools/make_diagrams.py` together with the figures
themselves, so it always matches the files in `figures/`.

**[Cheat sheet](cheatsheet.md).** Short reference snippets for the code
written most often in the book: pandas, SQL in DuckDB, scikit-learn
pipelines and evaluation, FastAPI and Pydantic, pytest, monitoring,
configuration through environment variables, ranking metrics and a PyTorch
training loop, with pointers to the chapters that explain each snippet.

## Running the labs

**[Before you start](before-you-start.md).** A short checklist to complete
before Chapter 1: opening a lab in Colab, running its setup cell, deciding
where artifacts are kept, and the knowledge the book assumes and does not
assume.

**[Setup](setup.md).** The two ways of running the labs, in Google Colab with
nothing installed or in a local Python environment, together with the
requirements files for each part, editable installation, exact version pins,
and a short test that the installation works.

**[Working in Colab](colab.md).** How Colab runtimes behave, what is lost when
a runtime is recycled, and the three ways the book protects work against that
loss (Google Drive, a downloaded archive, and deterministic rebuilding by the
catch-up cell). It also covers GPU runtimes for Part III and running a local
server inside Colab.

**[Troubleshooting](troubleshooting.md).** Common failures, organised by what
the reader observes: environment and Colab problems, data and SQL errors,
model artifacts, the API, tests, and the PyTorch and GPU problems of
Parts II and III. Each entry states the cause and the fix. Every lab also ends
with a troubleshooting table specific to that chapter.

## Planning and self-assessment

**[Study plan](study-plan.md).** A schedule for the whole book, organised by
chapter ranges and three-hour sessions, with the capstones, regular drills and
mock interviews placed between the parts, and shorter plans for readers with
less time.

**[Interpreting the diagnostic](diagnostic.md).** How to read the result of
Chapter 1, the skills diagnostic: converting the score in each of its five
areas into a rating, and the chapters that deserve the most attention for
each kind of result.

**[Readiness rubric](readiness-rubric.md).** A rubric for judging, from
evidence, whether a skill can be exercised independently. It rates three
abilities (explaining, coding and troubleshooting) in each area that the book
teaches, describes the level that each part aims at, and gives three
self-test drills for producing the evidence.

**[Interview questions](interview-questions.md).** Questions grouped by topic,
each followed by the points that a complete answer contains. They are meant
for practice aloud, after an attempt and before reading the points.

## How the book was checked

**[Verification](verification.md).** An exact record of what was executed
when the book was built, in which environments and with which library
versions, and of what was not executed (in particular Google Colab itself and
any GPU).

**[About the solutions](solutions.md).** How the worked solutions are
organised: one `solutions.md` per chapter with written answers, reference code
in `solutions.py` that is tested against the lab's automatic checks, the
confidence marker attached to each answer, and the guided solutions of the
capstone projects.
